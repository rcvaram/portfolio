1. You have something

I’ve coded in Java for 4 years as a professional and 10+ years as a coder.

I solved multiple bugs and designed systems along the journey.

Ex: I designed the oh-taxi app uber clone system for Pranavan Nadarajah’s NextGen innovations.

My knowledge can help others because I have been through the same struggles which can be faced by many developers.

If you have experience in any technology, there are people out there who need your insight.

🚀 Pro Tip: Your recent bug fix could be someone else’s biggest headache. Write about it!

2. Get someone to Read

If no one's is listening, what's the point of talking? People will say —that writing is good for you—once you start to write, you will understand clearly. Thats true. However, without fuel, you can’t drive a car. The same thing applies here as well. Without enough engagement, you will not be motivated.

You can see in my notification panel that I have more than 100 notifications that I need to go through; however, it's the fuel for me. I always respond to the people who write a response on my blog.

Here’s how I build engagement:

✅ Respond to comments — every interaction builds your audience.

✅ Read & engage with others — Medium is a community, not a solo game.

✅ Network with fellow writers — some of my favourites include Heinz Marbaise, Isa Karabocek, octree, Rasathurai Karan, Charini Peiris, Bergini Francis and Sabesan Sathananthan

💡 Key Takeaway: More engagement = more visibility = more earnings.

3. Don’t talk about theories

Nobody searches for “Intro to Java” on Medium. If they need theory, they go to ChatGPT or Google.

Developers want:

✅ Working solutions to real-world problems

✅ Error fixes with clear explanations

✅ Code examples that work

❌ Bad Idea: Writing generic theory like “What is Polymorphism in Java?
✅ Good Idea: Writing actionable fixes like “How I Fixed a Java Memory Leak in Production”

4. Be Direct — No Fluff, No Storytelling

I’m not a storyteller and don’t pretend to be one. You are a DEV right I know you come to see my solution, not the story.

🚀 My Writing Rule: If a Java newbie can’t understand it, I rewrite it.

Short. Simple. Direct.
That’s how you keep developers reading.

5. Keep It Simple and Focused

The longer your article, the more readers drop off. Extra words = lost readers.

Writing Tip: If a sentence doesn’t add value, delete it.

✅ Use simple, direct language.

✅ Avoid unnecessary explanations.

✅ Get straight to the solution.

6. Give What Your Followers Love

Once you start writing, you’ll notice what resonates with your audience.

I create multiple series based on what my followers love.

  • 📌 REST Api Best Practieses
  • 📌 System Design
  • 📌 Java Fun Challenges
  • 📌 Java Guidelines
💡 Pro Tip: Look at your most popular articles — double down on what works!

Ready to Make Your First $300?

You don’t need to be an expert or an influencer to start writing.

✔ Write about what you know.
✔ Solve real problems.
✔ Be ruthlessly helpful.

🚀 Your next $300 article? It’s in the last bug you fixed. Go write it!

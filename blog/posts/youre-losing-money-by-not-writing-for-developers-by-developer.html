You’re Losing Money by Not Writing — For Developers By a Developer

20M out of 27M developers don’t make passive income from their knowledge. You’re a developer. You code. You solve problems. But can you make $100/month writing about it?

If you are not a medium member, read it here.

This is not my regular technical blog.
Spoiler Alert!! Not every Article will hit $100 But You can get Loyal Followers? They are gold.

1. Know your Sht & Own it

I’ve coded in Java for 4 years and solved many real-world bugs.

My knowledge can help others because I have been through the same struggles that many developers face.

If you have experience in any technology, there are people out there who need your insight. It may be a blog on Medium or a video on YouTube.

Your Experience = Somone else’s lifeline.
Dont Overthink it Write about what you actually Know

2. Build an Audience

Your knowledge is useless if no one is listening.

Back in 2022, I started writing technical articles, and over time, I gained around 1,000 followers.

These followers stuck with me even when I didn’t post anything in 2023. Why?

  • They are not casual readers, they are developers like you.
  • They need my fixes, not my life story.
If you consistently share valuable insights, you’ll attract an audience that truly cares about what you have to say.

3. Solve Real Problems (Skip the theory)

Nobody cares about “Intro to Java”. People google specific errors.

Think about the problems you have faced in your career. Chances are, other developers are struggling with the same things.

If you solve a real-world problem and share your solution, developers will be drawn to your work.

Your Pain points = their pain points → Solve those.
Writing about generic topics won’t help; addressing real pain points will.

4. Be Direct — No Fluff, No Storytelling

I’m not a storyteller and don’t pretend to be one.

My focus is on delivering clear, actionable content. Developers don’t have time for fluff — they need solutions.

That’s why my content is simple and direct, making it accessible even for those who may struggle with English.

Devs are busy. Respect their time

5. Keep It Simple and Focused

Extra word = lost readers.

My rule: If a Java newbie can’t get what I am saying, I will rewrite it.

That’s why I always maintain a simple and direct tone. Clarity and conciseness should be your top priorities.

Clarity >>>>>> Creativity → Always…

6. Give What Your Followers Love

Once you gain followers, you will understand why they follow you.

For me, my audience follows me for my Java articles and knowledge. So, I give them exactly that with my 100% effort.

This commitment ensures that I don’t lose a single follower.

7. Find the Right Publications to Expand Your Reach

If you want to grow your audience even further, look for relevant publications where you can share your content.

I found JavaRevisited Publication (shoutout to javinpaul). It boosts my reach. Submit there. Free traffic.

8. Use ChatGPT to be your Editor and Not your writer

I use AI for

  • Fixing Grammar
  • Simplifying awkward sentences.
There’s no shame in leveraging AI to explain your content in better way.
Your Voice = Your Brand

You don’t need to be an expert or an influencer to succeed.

* Write What you know
* Solbe Real Problems
* Be ruthlessly helpful

Key Takeaways:

Your Experience > Fancy credentials
Loyal Followers > Casual Readers
Solve Specific problems, not general ones.
Use AI as a tool, not a crutch.
Need Examples? Check my Profile. I practise what I say…
Your next $100 article? It’s finding in that bug you fixed last week go write it…

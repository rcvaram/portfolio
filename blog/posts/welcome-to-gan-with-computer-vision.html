What is GANs?

Ian Goodfellow introduced generative adversarial networks(GANs) in 2014. It was proposed as an alternative to variational autoencoders (VAEs) which learn the later spaces of images, to generate synthetic images.

Basically, GANs aimed to create artificial images that couldn’t distinguish with real ones.

Best Example For How GAN Works 

Imagine there’s an ambitious young criminal who wants to counterfeit money and sell to a mobster who specializes in handling counterfeit money.

At first, the young counterfeiter is not good, and our expert mobster tells him. he’s money is way off from looking real.

Slowly he gets better and makes a goof ‘copy’ every so often. The mobster tells him when it’s okay.

After some time, both the little boy and expert mobster get better at their jobs, and now they have created almost real looking but fake money.


Basically, what we described above is how GAN works. GANs comprises of two types of networks a generator and a discriminator.

The purpose of the Generator Network is to take a random image initialization and decode it into a synthetic image.

The purpose of the Discriminator Network is, take this input and predict whether this image came from a real dataset or synthetic.

Two antagonistic networks that are contesting each other. The two components are called.

  1. Generator Network → In our example, this was our little boy.
  2. Discriminator Network → , The mobster

How to train GANs

Training GANs is notoriously tricky. In gradient descent, We can change our weights to reduce our loss. In GANs, every weight change changes the entire balance of our dynamic system. In GANs, we are not seeking to minimize loss but finding an equilibrium between our two opposing networks.

GAN Training Process

  1. Input randomly generated noisy images into Generator Network to create a sample image.
  2. Take some real images from the actual data and mix it with some of the generated synthetic images.
  3. Input these mixed images to Discriminator who will learn then, be trained on this mixed set, and will update its weights accordingly.
  4. Make some more fake images and input it into the Discriminator, but labelled as all are real. This is done to train the generator. We have frozen the weights fo the discriminator at this stage (Discriminator learning stops). And We use feedback from the discriminator to now update the weights of the generator. This is how we teach both out Generator( to make better artificial images) and Discriminator to get better at detecting falsies.

Again, Remember that GANs are basically two opposing networks that are sort of opposing each other. Generic Adversarial Network, so Adversarial means two or more agents play an opposing game with each other. That means your loss is my gain like Tic -Tac -Toe.

In GANs, We have two adversarial networks, the generator and the discriminator where each network is being trained to win over the there. The generator tries to make better fakes to beat the Discriminator while Discriminator learns to get better at detecting fakes.

I Studied Naval Ravikant’s Mental Models—Here's What Actually Matters

3 min read | Part 1 of a series

Most of us read books to collect ideas. very few of us try to understand how the author thinks. That difference changes everything.

Whenever we read any book, there is one important thing we must learn from it.

Instead of thinking about what the author is saying, we should think, "How does this author think before saying this?”

If we understand how the author thinks, we can learn that thinking pattern, model it, and achieve similar results.

Learning how they think is called a mental model.

Wealth according to NAVAL

Wealth is not about earning more money.

Wealth is about protecting your freedom and peace of mind.

If unexpected events happen in the future, your ability to handle them wisely is what defines wealth.

STEP 1: UNDERSTAND WHAT WEALTH IS

Most people think having a lot of money means being wealthy. But money is not wealth.

If your income comes from your time or effort, you are not truly wealthy.

Wealth is something that earns money even when you are not working.

Examples:

  • A business that runs without you.
  • content that earns continuous investments
  • Writing a book
  • Creating Software
  • Making youtube videos
If you can earn while sleeping, that is wealth.

If something can be replicated and sold multiple times, it becomes wealth.

If you do something once and earn repeatedly from it, that is wealth.

REPLICABLE WORK = WEALTH

STEP 2: Specific Knowledge

Specific knowledge is something.

  • Natural to you
  • Hard to teach others
  • Difficult to explain

Example: You may do something easily, but when someone asked to explain how, you struggle. That is specific knowledge.

To find it, ask:

  1. What do I do easily that others find difficult?
  2. What do people appreciate me for?

Your life experiences have already built this skill.

This is just the foundation. The next part goes deeper into leverage and decision-making models.

STEP 3: Accountability

Take full responsibility for your life and work.

When you take responsibility:

People trust you. You build reputation. Trust leads to wealth.

STEP 4: No Competition

If your work is truly unique, there will be no competition.

Your work should come from:

  • Your personality
  • Your emotions
  • Your unique thinking
If others can copy you easily, you are replaceable

STEP 5: Iteration (FAIL FAST)

  1. Try many ideas.
  2. Execute quickly.
  3. Fail quickly.

Failure is not a side effect. It is part of the process.

Remove failures and success will remain.

STEP 6: Productivize yourself

Turn your identity into a product.

That means:

  • Scale your knowledge.
  • Reach many people
  • Earn without your present.

Example: 
Teaching one person is not scalable. Creating a video or content is scalable.

Most people chase money. Naval focuses on freedom.

The difference is not in effort — it’s in how you think.

This is Part 1 of a series on Naval’s mental models. Part 2 will break down how to use these ideas in real decisions.

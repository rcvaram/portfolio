I learnt the lesson

Before executing an unfamiliar repository, understand what can execute beyond the application code you’re reviewing.

I’ve documented the investigation, evidence, and practical checks developers can perform when handling coding assignments from unfamiliar sources.

I would like to convey something to our developer community.

The job market is tough right now. I know many of us are willing to follow the instruction a recuireter gives us because we do not want to miss the opporunity.

But before you run a command, download a file, click a link or extract a zip,

pause for a moment and ask yourself.

“What exactly will happen when I do this activity?”

I have experienced a situation where a simple Git command could have triggered something I wasn’t expecting yesterday.

The reason I caught it was a small gut feeling that told me to look twice.

That feeling is built from years of working with software like seeing patterns, understanding how systems behave, and noticing when something doesn’t quite make sense.

Don’t ignore it.

If something feels wrong, pause. Investigate. Ask questions.

Understand what you’re about to execute.

It might take five extra minutes.

Those five minutes could save you from a much bigger problem.

Please share/repost this with developers who are currently doing take-home assignments.

Are you in Digital Jail? — DI. DAY

Some people came with Windows laptops.By the end of the event, they had switched to open-source systems. That single moment said the whole message.

Today in Munich, I went to aDigital Independence Day tech event. I didnt feel it is not just a tech event. It hit me with the remainder of something many developers already know but often ignore: the trade-off between convenience and control.

Many developers like me start with Linux, especially during university years. The terminal feels like own-hand. Systems are transparent and flexible. But once professional work begins, things change. Company requirements, specific tools, and team environments often push developers toward Windows.

The switch feels necessary, and over time, it becomes routine. This has literally happened to me.

Most modern tools are easy to use specifically from tech-giants such as microsoft, google, meta and Apple. The side effect is they slowly reduce how much control users have over their own systems.

The session is done by Roger. who brought to the audience cleary about how we dont have independece in digital world.

What stood out during the event was not just the comparison between Linux and Windows, but the mindset behind it.

Linux was presented as a system that gives back control.

  • predictable behavior
  • clean updates
  • better privacy,
  • flexibility.

Bring independence to our digital identity.

At the same time, the limitations were acknowledged. Some professional tools still depend on Windows, and initial setup can take effort.

Five participants including me switched their operating systems from Windows to open source alternatives during the event itself.

They were guided step by step by the organizing team. Special credit goes to Roger for delivering a clear and engaging presentation. Sebastian and Nick worked closely with participants, ensuring everything went smoothly and that people felt confident during the transition.

The concept of the four freedoms

  • running
  • studying
  • modifying
  • sharing software

Event reframed software is something to understand and control.

Several small but important habits were also highlighted.

  • Do not fear the terminal.
  • Read error messages carefully.
  • Use package managers instead of random downloads.
  • Learn small commands that save time.

These are simple practices and, they shape how effectively a system is used.

By the end of the event, one thing became clear.

Starting small. Using Linux again where possible. Keeping other systems only where necessary. Reducing dependency step by step.
